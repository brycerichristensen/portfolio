HLS Mission Analysis & Visualization

During my time at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC) in Huntsville, Alabama, I supported the Human Landing System (HLS) program focused on two main technical initiatives:

  1. Terrain-Relative Navigation (TRN) in-the-loop co-simulation capabilities
  2. Automated telemetry analysis pipelines for SpaceX Orbital Flight Tests (OFT)

Terrain-Relative Navigation (TRN) In-The-Loop Co-Simulation

Precision lunar landing requires real-time optical matching between onboard descent cameras and digital elevation models. Previously, TRN visualization operated in an offline "Playback Mode," passing static pre-set .csv telemetry files through Python scripts into Unreal Engine to export individual frame sequences.

I helped transition this architecture into a closed-loop Co-Simulation (In-The-Loop) framework. By linking the GNC simulation directly to an Unreal Engine executable via a custom Simulink-to-UE block, the system streams state vectors in real time, receives synthetic optical frames back, and dynamically adjusts the landing trajectory during simulation runs.

The top diagram shows the Simulink side of the connection bridge, while the bottom diagram illustrates how data passed into Unreal Engine is acted on to dynamically update vehicle position and rotation.

Optical Descent Co-Simulation Demonstration

Note: Lunar elevation models are accurate, but trajectory profiles are non-representative of actual landers, and camera parameters are not tuned to flight hardware.

This sequence consists of individual rendered optical images stitched together at a specified framerate. As each image is generated inside Unreal Engine, it is fed directly into the TRN algorithm to calculate state estimates and determine vehicle position and attitude in real time.

This real-time optical loop allows GNC engineers to evaluate feature-tracking confidence under dynamic solar lighting angles and crater terrain conditions during powered descent.

Starship Orbital Flight Test (OFT) Telemetry Automation

Evaluating flight data from SpaceX Starship test launches previously required a manual 2 to 4 day post-flight process involving separate MATLAB plot generation, video splicing in DaVinci Resolve, and manual state inspection (shown in the top "Previous State" branch of the flowchart).

To streamline this workflow, I engineered an automated processing suite (highlighted inside the red bounding box in the pipeline diagram):

  • SPAR (State Parser with Anomaly Recognition): Automatically parses raw .mat telemetry files in 1–2 seconds to detect state transitions and dynamic flight anomalies.
  • VISOR (Video Integration, Splicing, and Overlay Renderer): Slices Unreal Engine renders, overlays synchronized telemetry graphics, and renders finished analysis videos in under an hour (GUI interface previewed on the right).

This pipeline reduced total turnaround time down to a single day, establishing a path toward 2–4 hour automated turns via standalone Unreal Engine executables.

Auxiliary Propulsion: RCS Thruster Sizing

To support broader GNC maneuvering and attitude control requirements, I served as the GNC expert on an intern team tasked with performing preliminary sizing and trade studies for a Reaction Control System (RCS) thruster block. Working alongside two other interns, our team owned the design process from initial velocity increment (Δv) requirements through preliminary architecture layout.

The system was evaluated for versatile translational and attitude maneuvers across various orbital environments, whether operating in Earth orbit, lunar transfer trajectories, or proximity operations. Our team focused primarily on sizing liquid methane and liquid oxygen (methalox) thruster configurations—evaluating injector orifice geometries, spark torch igniter integration, feed system pressure drops, and film cooling thermal management. We also conducted comparative baseline sizing calculations for hydrolox (liquid hydrogen / liquid oxygen) architectures to analyze mass-efficiency and impulse performance trade-offs.
